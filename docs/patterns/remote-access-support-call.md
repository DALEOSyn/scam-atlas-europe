# Pattern brief: `remote-access-support-call`

**Chapter:** 9 Stockholm (one "support" call previews the finale, LO-15) and 10 Brussels (finale boss, GDD §18) · **Status:** draft for review (T-3.10r; Stockholm preview scope; the Brussels research may extend it) · **Researched:** 2026-11-19 · **Owner:** fraud-researcher

A caller from "your bank", "IT support", "the provider" or an investment "adviser" asks you to install an app or share your screen so they can "fix", "protect" or "help". With control of the device, they can see codes and approve or make payments. Defender level only: no product names, no install or connection steps.

## 1. Name and aliases
- **English:** tech-support scam; remote-access scam; fake support call.
- **Swedish:** *fjärråtkomst*, *dela din skärm* (share your screen) [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv].
- **Game label:** a fictional "support" caller and a fictional, clearly non-functional "helper" app screen (Red Line 2). No real remote-access product names or icons (several real ones appear in official sources: research only).
- **Evidence:**
  - EU: tech-support fraud is among the most common typologies reported by Member States [eu-europol-iocta-2026]; fake remote support [eu-epc-threats-2025].
  - ESAs: "Never install remote-access programs or share your screen"; banks never ask you to [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv].
  - SE: "Never let someone you don't know access your computer remotely" [se-konsumentverket-investment-fraud].
  - IE: investment "advisers" ask victims to install remote-access software; "NEVER allow anyone to access your computer" [ie-garda-crimecall-investment-2024].
  - BE/DE: trading-platform fraudsters offer to take over the device [be-fsma-trading-platforms-2025]; "Do not give anyone access to your devices via remote maintenance software" [de-evz-online-trading-scam-2026].
  - PL: fake consultants ask for remote-access apps [pl-cert-fake-consultants].
  - CH (supporting only): pop-up "locked screen" warnings with a number to call [ch-ncsc-fake-support-2024].

## 2. What the victim sees
1. A call (or a pop-up or message giving a number to call) about a problem: a virus, a hacked account, a suspicious payment, or "help" with an investment [ch-ncsc-fake-support-2024], [eu-epc-threats-2025], [ie-garda-crimecall-investment-2024].
2. A request to install an app from a store or link, or to share the screen, and to read out a code the app shows.
3. Then: "log in to your bank so I can check", or "approve this to secure your account".

**Levers:** authority and expertise; fear; helpfulness; the feeling of being looked after.

## 3. Red flags
1. **Anyone who contacted you asks you to install an app or share your screen** [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv], [se-konsumentverket-investment-fraud].
2. **They ask you to log in to your bank or approve something while they watch** [se-konsumentverket-investment-fraud], [se-polisen-telefonbedrageri].
3. **A pop-up or message tells you to call a number** for support [ch-ncsc-fake-support-2024] (supporting).
4. **Urgency** and staying on the line [se-polisen-anmal-telefonbedrageri].

## 4. The verification habit
**"Never let anyone who contacted you control or see your screen. Hang up and contact the company yourself, using a number from its official app, card or website."** [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv], [se-konsumentverket-investment-fraud], [ie-garda-crimecall-investment-2024]

## 5. Already caught? (generic)
1. **Take the device offline** (airplane mode) and stop using it for banking (help-screen first step `disconnect`; sources there).
2. **Call your bank now** on the number on your card [se-polisen-telefonbedrageri]; using another phone if you can is generic advice, not from this source.
3. **Change passwords** [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv]; doing it from a different device is generic advice, not from this source.
4. **Get the device checked** before using it again (generic advice, not from these sources; [pl-cert-fake-consultants] only says never to let anyone control your device remotely).
5. **Report to the police** (SE: 114 14) [se-polisen-telefonbedrageri].

## 6. Stockholm preview: design notes
- One short call that stops at the install request (the player hangs up), as a preview of the Brussels boss.
- Debrief layer 1: the habit above; no detail of what the app could do beyond "they could see and do what you can".
- Keep it separate from the e-ID beats so the two habits don't blur: e-ID = "don't approve what you didn't start"; remote = "don't let them in".
