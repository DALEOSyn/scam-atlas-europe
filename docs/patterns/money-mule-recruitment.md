# Pattern brief: `money-mule-recruitment`

**Chapter:** 10 Brussels (Noor's easy-money DM, GDD §18; LO-12, LO-18) · **Status:** draft for review (T-3.12r) · **Researched:** 2026-11-19 · **Owner:** fraud-researcher · **Audience:** 14+

Someone online offers easy money for "using your account": to receive money and pass it on, or to lend your bank card and PIN, or (the game's digital translation) your banking-app login or codes, "just for a few transfers". The money comes from other people's losses. The person whose account is used carries the risk.

**Framing:** shown only from the target's side. The player is the target of a trap, never the criminal. The brief covers no how-to: nothing on how money is moved, layered or cashed out, and nothing on how recruiters find or manage people.

## 1. Name and aliases
- **English:** money mule; "lending your account".
- **Dutch (BE/NL):** *geldezel*. Not quoted from an opened Dutch-language page this session; it is the common term.
- **French (BE):** *mule financière* [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr]; *mule d'argent* is also used (not quoted).
- **Game label:** a fictional recruiter profile on a fictional social app or on Kettle. No real platform names or logos (several are named in the sources: research only).

## 2. Countries and why
- **Belgium:**
  - The financial-sector federation runs a standing money-mule page aimed at young people [be-febelfin-money-mules].
  - It reported in 2023 that young Belgians are "particularly targeted" [be-febelfin-money-mules-2023].
  - It ran a 2025 campaign on scams in games and chat groups [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].
  - The Federal Police also warns about it [be-police-money-mules-2020] (older).
  - Survey figures exist (see §9); they are for designers only.
- **EU:**
  - Europol's EMMA campaign has a Belgian French-language flyer [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr] and other editions [eu-europol-mule-jobs], [eu-europol-mule-flyer-ie].
  - Targets include students, under-35s and minors.
  - Money mules also appear in the EPC threat report [eu-epc-threats-2025].

## 3. Channel and psychological levers
**Channel (digital only, RL8):** a DM on social media, a messenger chat, a social-media ad or pop-up, a fake job post, or a chat inside a game [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [be-febelfin-money-mules], [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].

The sources also describe offline recruitment near schools, stations and nightlife. That is out of scope (RL8) and is not shown.

**What the target sees:**
- A friendly, casual message, sometimes from someone they know, or who seems to be a friend of a friend. Danger "can come from close friends" [be-febelfin-money-mules-2023].
- The offer: easy money, quickly, for "a favour".
- Talk of what others have earned [be-febelfin-money-mules].

**Levers:**
- money, and wanting what others have;
- friendship and belonging ("it's a favour");
- reassurance ("perfectly safe") [be-febelfin-money-mules];
- urgency;
- later, fear: threats if you try to stop [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [be-febelfin-money-mules]. **Designer-only, not for screen (round 2, M2).** On screen, use only: "If anyone pressures you to keep going, that's a reason to get help now."

## 4. Red flags (numbered, concrete)
1. **Easy money you didn't ask for.** "Too good to be true" [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
2. **The "job" or "favour" uses your own bank account**: money comes in and you send it on [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [eu-europol-mule-jobs].
3. **They ask for your account number, bank card, PIN, banking-app login or codes.**
   - Card and PIN: [be-febelfin-money-mules], [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
   - Login details or codes: [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].
   - The "banking-app login" wording is the game's translation (see §10).
4. **It is all online, and no experience is needed** [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
5. **"It's perfectly safe" or "it's just a favour"** [be-febelfin-money-mules], [be-police-money-mules-2020].
6. **A bonus for bringing in friends** [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
7. **"Keep it between us"**, or pressure to decide now. This is a general scam lever, consistent with GDD slot 3; it is not specific to the mule sources.

## 5. The verification habit
**"Never let anyone use your account, card, banking app or codes, whoever asks and whatever they offer. If an offer involves your account, stop and talk to someone you trust."**

Sources:
- [be-febelfin-money-mules]: never share account, card or PIN; talk to someone you trust.
- [be-police-money-mules-2020]: "Ne prêtez jamais votre compte bancaire".
- [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025]: never share login details or codes.
- [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].

## 6. Why it matters (state once, factually, as the reason to get help)
**On-screen text (round 2, M1 and M2).** Use only these lines. The consequence line always comes with the next step:
- "Letting someone use your account like this is a crime, and the account holder can be held responsible. If it's already happened, getting help early is the right move."
- "If anyone pressures you to keep going, that's a reason to get help now."

**Designer background.** The sources agree on the consequences below for the person whose account is used. Items marked **NOT FOR SCREEN** must not appear in any player-facing text, in any form.
- **It is a crime.** You can be prosecuted, and penalties can include a fine, community service or prison [be-febelfin-money-mules], [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [be-police-money-mules-2020].
- **You may have to pay back the money** that went through your account [be-febelfin-money-mules], [be-police-money-mules-2020].
- **Your account can be blocked or closed.** Banks may refuse you an account, card or loan later [be-febelfin-money-mules], [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
- **NOT FOR SCREEN (designer-only, M2):** for under-18s, parents can be held liable [be-febelfin-money-mules], [be-police-money-mules-2020].
- **NOT FOR SCREEN (designer-only, M2):** the people behind it may threaten you if you try to stop [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [be-febelfin-money-mules]. On screen this becomes only the "pressures you to keep going" line above.
- **Your own account can be emptied** [be-febelfin-money-mules-2023], [be-police-money-mules-2020].

**Writer rules:**
- Use the on-screen consequence sentence once in the debrief, always followed by the next step (as above). Never use it alone.
- Don't add anything about whether the account holder knew where the money came from (round 2, M1).
- No list of punishments on screen, no prison imagery, and no shame.
- Repaying, account closure and refused accounts may be used in layer 2 only if a reviewer asks for more detail. Parents' liability and threats never go on screen (M2).

## 7. Already said yes? What to do (14+)
1. **Stop.** Don't move any more money and don't share anything else [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
2. **Tell a trusted adult today**: a parent, carer, teacher or someone you trust.
   - Source: "talk to someone you trust" [be-febelfin-money-mules].
   - The "today" and the list of adults are safeguarding framing for a 14+ audience, not from the source.
3. **Call your bank now.**
   - Use the number on your card or in the app; banks are reachable 24/7 [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].
   - Block the card [be-febelfin-money-mules].
   - In Belgium, Card Stop / Fraudstop on 078 170 170 can block cards and banking apps [be-febelfin-fraudstop-2026].
4. **Change your banking login and passcodes** if you shared them.
   - Supported by "never share login details or codes" [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].
   - The step itself is generic advice.
5. **Go to the police** [be-febelfin-money-mules], [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr].
   - Belgium: a local police station, ideally with a trusted adult.
   - Police on Web needs a Belgian identity card [be-police-plainte-arnaque-en-ligne]. Whether minors can use it was not checked.
6. **Report the recruiter's account to the app or platform** [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [eu-europol-mule-flyer-ie].
7. **Warn friends** who may have had the same offer [eu-europol-mule-flyer-be-fr], [be-febelfin-money-mules].

**Debrief tone line (GDD §18):** "If you've already shared it, it's not too late: change your login, call your bank on the number on your card, and tell a trusted adult". This is consistent with the sources above.

**MAP wording check:** MAP's draft line includes "you won't be in trouble for asking".
- No source supports a promise about legal outcomes. The sources say mules can be prosecuted and may have to repay.
- **Recommendation:** replace it with **"Getting help early is the right move. Asking for help is never the wrong thing to do."** This keeps the encouragement without a legal promise.

## 8. Variations by country
- **BE:**
  - Social media and messaging apps; game chats and fake coin offers [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].
  - The ask is phrased as account + card + PIN [be-febelfin-money-mules].
  - Card Stop / Fraudstop 078 170 170 [be-febelfin-fraudstop-2026].
  - **Warning:** Febelfin's money-mule page still lists Card Stop's old 070 344 344. That number is outdated; never use it.
- **EU:**
  - EMMA flyers per country (BE French edition, IE edition, generic job-offer flyer).
  - Same red flags; fake job offers that "only need your account" [eu-europol-mule-jobs].
  - Links to `task-job-scam.md` (Amsterdam).

## 9. Designer context (never on screen)
- About 17% of 16-30s were willing to hand over card + PIN (2023 survey) [be-febelfin-money-mules-2023].
- About 60% of young people approached as mules accepted (2025 survey) [be-febelfin-games-campaign-2025].
- 14% of young Belgians would share card + PIN for money [be-febelfin-phishing-2025].
- These come from different surveys and different questions: do not compare them or show them.

## 10. Game notes (Noor's DM, Brussels)
- **Show:** the DM, the casual tone, the "easy money" offer, the ask (banking-app login, card details or account access "just for a few transfers"), and a friendly follow-up.
- **Don't show:** what happens to money after it arrives, or any transfer mechanics.
- **Player choices that score well:**
  - tell Noor it's a trap and to tell a trusted adult (`ask_someone`);
  - report the account (`report`);
  - ignore or block (`ignore`).
- **If Noor already said yes:** the scene shows her getting help, in the §7 order: stop, tell an adult she trusts, call the bank, police (STORY §5.2).
  - No scene calls Noor's action a crime.
  - The consequence sentence (§6) lives only in the general debrief, never in Noor's scene or her dialogue.
- **Accuracy note (keep, MAP):** the sources name account, card and PIN, and (Febelfin 2025) login details and codes. "Banking-app login" is the game's digital translation, not a quoted statistic.

## 12. Confidence
- **High:**
  - the red flags, the consequences and the "what to do" steps;
  - consistent across Febelfin, the Federal Police and Europol.
- **Not verified:**
  - **Minors:** how Belgian law treats under-18 mules in practice (youth justice) is not covered by the sources. Make no claims about it on screen. Parents' liability is designer-only (M2). Police on Web for minors was not checked either.
  - **Dutch alias:** the term was not quoted from an opened source.
  - **Banking-app login:** the wording is a translation. No opened source says recruiters ask specifically for banking-app logins, though Febelfin 2025 warns against sharing login details and codes.
  - **The 2020 Federal Police page** is older; it is used only alongside current sources.
