# Pattern brief: `recovery-scam`

**Chapter:** 8 Dublin (GDD §18: recovery-scam offers to victims) and the Alex arc ending C (GDD §10.3, LO-13/LO-16); cross-cutting in every money-loss debrief (MAP crosscutting point 4) · **Status:** draft for review (T-3.10r) · **Researched:** 2026-11-19 · **Owner:** fraud-researcher

Related brief: `investment-ad-fake-platform.md` (the first scam).

After a loss, the victim is contacted by someone who offers to get the money back, for a fee paid first. It is a second scam aimed at people who were already scammed.

## 1. Name and aliases
- **English:** recovery scam; recovery room; "fund recovery" fraud.
- **Local names:**
  - SE: *räddningshjälp* ("rescue help"; the consumer bureau calls it "ett fortsatt bedrägeri", a continued fraud) [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp].
  - BE/FR: *recovery rooms* [be-fsma-dashboard-2026-h1].
- **Game labels:** a fictional "recovery agency", "solicitor" or "regulator unit" with `.example` addresses and masked numbers. Never a real authority's name or look (Red Line 1).
- **Why Ireland:**
  - The banks' federation warned in 2024 that victims are re-targeted with upfront "retainer" or "processing" fees [ie-bpfi-recovery-2024].
  - The central bank's November 2025 campaign: "Never pay fees for recovering lost funds" [ie-cbi-fraud-landscape-2025].
  - Garda advice: "recovery experts" are "usually just an extension of the fraud" [ie-garda-crimecall-investment-2024].
  - EU: victims are "relentlessly re-victimised" [eu-europol-iocta-2026].

## 2. Channel and psychological levers
**What the victim sees:** a call, email, message or social post, soon after a loss or months later:
- someone who knows about the loss and claims they can trace or freeze the money [ie-bpfi-recovery-2024], [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp];
- they pose as a lawyer, a recovery firm, a "regulator", the police or another authority, sometimes with a spoofed number [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams], [eu-esas-crypto-factsheet-2025], [de-evz-online-trading-scam-2026], [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp];
- **the ask:** a fee, "tax", "retainer" or "administration fee" first [ie-bpfi-recovery-2024], [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams], [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp]; or to log in to your bank or hand over details "so we can return the money" [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp].

**Levers:** hope and desperation after a loss [ie-cbi-fraud-landscape-2025]; borrowed authority; shame (wanting to fix it quietly); urgency.

## 3. Red flags a normal person can spot
1. **They contacted you** about money you lost. Ignore unsolicited recovery offers [ie-cbi-fraud-landscape-2025].
2. **A fee, tax or deposit comes first** [ie-bpfi-recovery-2024], [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams], [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp].
3. **They claim to be an authority, police or a lawyer** and still want money or your bank login [eu-esas-crypto-factsheet-2025], [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp].
4. **They know details of your loss.** Fraudsters share victims' details with each other [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams].
5. **They promise success.** "It is highly unlikely you will ever recover your money" from the original scam this way [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams].

## 4. The verification habit
**"Anyone who contacts you offering to get your money back for a fee is almost certainly running a second scam. Don't pay; go to your bank and the police yourself."** [ie-bpfi-recovery-2024], [ie-cbi-fraud-landscape-2025], [se-konsumentverket-investment-fraud]

- If someone claims to be an authority, hang up and contact that body on details you look up yourself (as in `spoofed-authority-call.md`).
- Only your bank can raise a card chargeback; outside firms can't do it for you [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp] (SE; designer context).

## 5. Already caught? (generic + Ireland, verified 2026-11-19)
1. Stop paying [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams].
2. Call your bank on the number on your card or in its app [ie-ccpc-investment-scams].
3. Gave a login or approved something? Change passwords and tell the bank [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv].
4. Report to the Gardaí and the Central Bank (IE) [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams]; keep everything they sent.
5. Never promise a refund on screen.

## 6. Variations by country
- **IE:** "retainer"/"processing" fees [ie-bpfi-recovery-2024]; fictitious government agencies [ie-cbi-avoiding-scams].
- **SE:** fake "supervisor" calls with spoofed numbers that ask you to log in to your bank; "agents" for several victims [se-konsumenternas-raddningshjalp].
- **BE/DE:** recovery offers after trading-platform fraud; posing as regulators, mediators or solicitors [be-fsma-trading-platforms-2025], [de-evz-online-trading-scam-2026].
- **EU:** "being scammed once does not prevent you from being scammed again" [eu-esas-crypto-factsheet-2025], [eu-esas-ai-scams-factsheet-sv].

## 7. Genuine twin
Your own bank, which you called on the number on your card, asks you to file a police report and keep evidence, and asks for no fee.
