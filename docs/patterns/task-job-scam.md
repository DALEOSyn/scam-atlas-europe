# Pattern brief: `task-job-scam`

**Chapter:** 2 (Amsterdam) · **Status:** draft for review (phase 3, ch2 step 1) · **Researched:** 2026-11-19 · **Owner:** fraud-researcher

## 1. Name and aliases
- **English:** task scam; "easy work" / work-from-home job scam; review-writing job scam; pay-to-earn job scam.
- **Local names:** NL: *vacaturefraude* (job fraud, the umbrella term) [nl-fraudehelpdesk-fraudevormen]; *thuiswerkvacatures* ("work-from-home vacancies") [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025]; news uses "telefoontjes die makkelijk werk beloven" ("calls promising easy work") [nl-nos-easy-work-2025]. Fraudehelpdesk's glossary has no separate "task scam" entry [nl-fraudehelpdesk-fraudevormen].
- **Where it is prominent and why:**
  - **Netherlands:** Fraudehelpdesk has run a dedicated alert since May 2025 (updated Oct 2025, Jan 2026) and a standing advice page [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden]; the government awareness platform warned in May 2025 [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025]; in Dec 2025 the police contacted ~300 people who were sending crypto to scammers, a few of them in job fraud where they "worked from home" writing reviews [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025].
  - Scale is known only via news: NOS (6 Aug 2025), citing Fraudehelpdesk, reported 2025 reports already 10× the whole of 2024 by August [nl-nos-easy-work-2025]. **Part-year, news-reported; designer context only.** Fraudehelpdesk's full-year review does not itemise it [nl-fraudehelpdesk-2025].
  - **EU-wide:** the EPC describes task scams with small early payouts [eu-epc-threats-2025].
  - Why it works: the work looks real and easy, the first small payout "proves" it, and each new payment is framed as the last step to getting your money.

## 2. Channel and psychological levers
**Channel (victim's view):**
1. **First contact:** in NL usually an unsolicited **phone call**, often a recorded message, or an **SMS**, from someone claiming to be a staffing agency, a well-known job site or an HR department [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-nos-easy-work-2025]. Social-media ads and job posts are also reported [nl-nos-easy-work-2025]. (Real job-site names in the sources are references only.)
2. **Move to the messenger:** you are asked to save the number and continue in the chat app; a friendly "recruiter" or "mentor" guides you [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden].
3. **The offer:** remote work, no training, just a phone and internet; e.g. €100–300 for about an hour a day; tasks are rating or writing reviews of products, services or businesses, or "trading" crypto [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025], [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025].
4. **The task site:** you work on a website or app that shows tasks and a growing "balance". A small payout early on builds trust [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [eu-epc-threats-2025].
5. **The turn:** you must **pay in first** to get tasks; then you're told you haven't done enough tasks and must pay more to avoid a "fine" or finish the set; or a large balance can be released only after "fees". The amounts keep growing and nothing comes back [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden], [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025]. Payments may go in crypto [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025].
6. **Possible side-asks:** a copy of your ID [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden]; in the wider job-fraud family, moving money through your account, opening accounts or forwarding goods [nl-fraudehelpdesk-fraudevormen] (see §6, money mules).

**Levers:** easy money and flexibility; a friendly mentor; proof by small payout; sunk cost ("pay this one fee and you get it all back"); fear of a "fine" or losing the balance; shame, which keeps people paying and silent [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025], [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025].

## 3. Red flags a normal person can spot
1. **You have to pay to earn.** Any deposit, top-up, fee, "unlock" or "fine" before you get paid [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025].
2. **A job offer you didn't ask for**, by call, recorded message, SMS, messenger or social media [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden].
3. **Too good to be true:** high pay for little time, no skills or training needed [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025].
4. **Pressure to save an unknown number and move to the messenger** [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden].
5. **A balance you can see but can't withdraw** without paying more first [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden].
6. **Reasons why you get less, or nothing** ("not enough tasks done", "pay a fine first") [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025].
7. **"Tasks" that involve crypto trading**, or paying in crypto [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025].
8. **A request for an ID copy** before any real contract or interview [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden].

*Note for writers:* a small early payout is **not** a sign the job is real. It is part of the trick [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [eu-epc-threats-2025].

## 4. The verification habit
**"A real job pays you. It never asks you to pay in. If an 'employer' wants a deposit, a fee or a top-up before you can earn or withdraw, stop: it's a scam."** ("Betaal niet voor werk waarvoor u juist betaald moet worden.") [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden], [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025]

Supporting habits:
- **Don't answer unsolicited job offers** and don't save unknown numbers or move to a chat app because a stranger asks [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025].
- **Look the company up yourself** (its own site and contact details, not the links in the chat) and **talk it through with someone you trust** before paying anything [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025]. For teens: a parent, teacher or other trusted adult.
- Never send an ID copy or your bank login to a "recruiter" [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden].

## 5. Already caught? (generic + NL specifics verified 2026-11-19)
1. **Stop paying.** No "last fee" will release the money; the amounts keep growing [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025].
2. **Contact your bank** right away (and the crypto provider if you paid that way) [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025], [nl-veiligbankieren-meld-fraude].
3. **Keep evidence:** chats, the site address, payment records.
4. **Contact Fraudehelpdesk** for personal advice and to report (088 786 73 72 or the online form) [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025], [nl-fraudehelpdesk-contact-en], [nl-rijksoverheid-fraudehelpdesk-contact].
5. **Report to the police** (aangifte). There is no dedicated job-fraud page; the general fraud route says call 0900-8844 or go to a station [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025], [nl-politie-ik-ben-opgelicht], [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025]. Online reporting for this type was not confirmed this session.
6. **If you sent a copy of your ID:** report it to the Centraal Meldpunt Identiteitsfraude (CMI) at RvIG [nl-fraudehelpdesk-baan-aangeboden], [nl-rvig-cmi-id-copy].
7. **Beware of "recovery" offers** that ask for a deposit to get your money back; police say these are often linked to the same criminals [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025].
8. **Support:** Slachtofferhulp Nederland, 0900-0101 [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025].
9. **If anyone asked you to receive or pass on money through your account**, stop and tell your bank and the police (money mules: see the Brussels chapter) [nl-veiligbankieren-geldezels], [eu-europol-mule-jobs].
- Tone line: anyone can fall for this; there is no need to feel ashamed [nl-veiliginternetten-thuiswerk-2025], [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025]. Teens: tell a parent or trusted adult first; getting help early is the right move.

## 6. Variations, local hooks and links to other patterns
- **NL hooks (generic wording for the game):** a recorded-message call or SMS from a fictional "staffing agency"; "save this number and message us on Kettle"; a fictional review-task site (`.example` domain) showing tasks, a balance and a "top up to continue" button that does nothing real (Red Line 2); payment in to the platform by a fictional bank transfer or a generic "crypto top-up". No real job site, staffing agency, crypto platform or payment app name or look (Red Line 1).
- **Teen / student relevance (14+ audience):**
  - The scam promises side income with no experience, which fits students. **But no NL source opened this session names teens or students as task-scam targets**; the SMS variant even asks for age 20+ [nl-fraudehelpdesk-vacature-alert-2025]. Relevance is *inferred*.
  - Related evidence about young people: Dutch banks' survey of 18–25-year-olds found over 2 in 3 had seen social-media ads or messages offering quick money [nl-nvb-geldezels-jongeren-2024] (mule context; designer-only figure); Europol notes minors drawn into laundering via "get-rich" social-media schemes [eu-europol-iocta-2026].
  - Game framing: the player or a student friend gets the offer; the lesson is the pay-to-earn rule.
- **Money-mule link (Brussels owns money mules):** Dutch sources put money-mule recruitment under the same job-fraud umbrella: fake jobs where you receive money and forward it for a cut [nl-fraudehelpdesk-fraudevormen], [nl-veiligbankieren-geldezels]; police say mules are "often young people", increasingly recruited via social media and ads [nl-politie-money-mules]; Europol's EMMA flyer warns of online "agent" jobs using your own bank account [eu-europol-mule-jobs]. **The Amsterdam scenario should not cover mules**; at most, the debrief can point ahead ("if a 'job' ever asks to use your bank account, that's a different trap: see Brussels").
- **Investment-scam overlap:** the same NL police case mixed job and investment fraud, both with a professional-looking personal dashboard showing growing returns [nl-politie-crypto-job-fraud-2025]. The "dashboard balance you can't withdraw" mechanic is shared with Dublin (ch8).

## 7. Genuine-twin notes
- Real jobs and side gigs do exist and some recruit through messages. A genuine twin: an offer that asks for an interview or a contract and **never asks you to pay**; pay goes *to* you, from the employer.
- A real employer may ask for ID at the contract stage for payroll; the scam tell is a **payment request** (or ID copy demanded by an unverified chat "recruiter" before any contract). Don't make "they asked for ID" the sole tell.
- Per CONTENT_GUIDE: the recruiter's display name, logo and "company" page are decoys (neutral wording). The deciding detail is the pay-in request.

## 8. Game-design notes (non-binding)
- Scenario arc: call/SMS teaser → messenger chat → task site (balance grows, one small payout) → "top up to unlock" → decision. Right move: refuse to pay in, check the company yourself, talk to someone.
- Red Line 8: the digital vectors are the call/SMS, the messenger chat and the fake site. Red Line 2: the task site is visibly in-game; nothing is submitted anywhere.
- Don't depict how the platform is built or run; show only what the player sees.
